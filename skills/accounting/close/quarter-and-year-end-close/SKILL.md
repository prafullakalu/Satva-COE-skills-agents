# Quarter-end and year-end close

Assumes the month-end-close checklist is complete. This skill adds the judgemental, estimate-heavy and statutory work that monthly closes skip. Jurisdiction-specific filing dates and rules vary: confirm with the local statutory calendar and a qualified adviser.

## Quarter-end additions
1. **Income tax provision.** Pre-tax book income x expected annual effective rate (interim method), adjusted for discrete items in the quarter. Tie the current tax payable to estimated payments made.
2. **Indirect tax returns** (sales tax/VAT/GST) filed or scheduled; control account agrees to returns.
3. **Estimates review.** Bad-debt allowance, inventory reserve, warranty, bonus, vacation accrual, contingent liabilities: re-measure and document the change in estimate.
4. **Balance sheet scrub.** Clear stale items: unapplied customer credits and vendor debits over 90 days, old uncashed cheques (unclaimed-property rules apply by jurisdiction), stale deposits.
5. **Equity and debt.** Interest accrual, covenant calculations (e.g. debt service coverage, current ratio) against facility terms, and any covenant waiver needed.
6. **Management and board reporting** (see financial-reporting-pack).

## Year-end additions
### A. Cut-off and counts
- Physical stock count or cycle-count roll-up as of year end; investigate variances over materiality; post shrinkage.
- Cut-off test: last 10 receipts/shipments before and first 10 after year end matched to invoices and bills.
- Confirm revenue cut-off against delivery/performance dates (revenue-recognition-606).

### B. Confirmations and third-party balances
- Bank confirmations requested for every account including closed-in-year accounts.
- Loan statements, lease schedules, merchant/payment-processor statements as of year end.
- Customer and vendor statement reconciliations for top balances.

### C. Estimates and provisions
Bad debts, obsolete stock, accrued bonuses/holiday, legal and tax contingencies, asset impairment indicators, onerous contracts. Each with calculation, source data and approver.

### D. Fixed assets
Full register to GL reconciliation, physical verification sample, impairment review, useful-life/residual-value review, disposals and fully depreciated assets still in use (fixed-assets-depreciation).

### E. Tax
Final income-tax provision and deferred tax (temporary differences x enacted rate; assess recoverability of any deferred tax asset). Contractor information returns (contractor-1099-reporting). Payroll year-end forms (payroll-accounting). Collect documents for the tax preparer (estimated-tax-and-tax-prep-organiser).

### F. Subsequent events
Review the period from year end to the date financials are issued. Adjusting events (e.g. customer bankruptcy confirming a receivable at year end, settlement of litigation) change the numbers; non-adjusting events (new acquisition, post-year-end loss from fire) need disclosure only. Get a written management representation of what was reviewed.

### G. Going concern
Forecast 12 months from the issue date (cash-flow-forecasting); document covenant headroom and funding plans. Flag material uncertainty to management.

### H. Roll-forward
- Close P&L accounts to retained earnings (most systems do this automatically: verify the retained earnings roll: opening RE + net income - dividends = closing RE).
- Opening balances of the new year = closing of the old: run a TB comparison.
- Lock the year (hard lock) after audit adjustments are posted; post audit adjustments dated in the old year with an "audit adjustment" tag.

## Year-end file contents (what the auditor or tax preparer will ask for)
TB with prior-year comparatives; GL detail; bank recs and confirmations; AR/AP agings; stock count sheets; fixed asset register and depreciation; loan, lease and equity schedules; payroll year-end reports; indirect tax returns; journal listing of manual entries over threshold; legal letters; minutes and approvals; prior-year management letter points and their status. See audit-support-pbc for the request-list format.

## Failure modes
- Estimating once and never revisiting: record each estimate with basis and compare to actual next year.
- Posting audit adjustments in the wrong period.
- Forgetting accrued but unpaid year-end bonuses and holiday pay.
- Treating post-year-end events as adjusting without evidence of conditions existing at year end.
- Tax provision prepared from pre-adjustment profit.

## Do not
Do not give tax or legal opinions; present calculations and flag items for the tax adviser. Do not finalise before management has reviewed and approved the estimates.

## Output
Year-end checklist with sign-offs, estimates schedule, adjusted TB, draft financial statements (financial-statement-preparation), subsequent-events memo, and the audit file index.
