# Reverse charge mechanism

LAST_VERIFIED: 2026-07-29
VOLATILE: the notified lists. Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate) (services) and
4/2017-CT(Rate) (goods) have been amended many times. Verify the current entry
before concluding that a supply is or is not under RCM.

## Why RCM deserves a dedicated screen

RCM liability is self-assessed with nothing auto-populating it — GSTR-3B Table
3.1(d) is blank until someone types in it. There is no supplier filing a GSTR-1
that would reveal an omission, so an unrecorded RCM liability can sit undetected
for years and then surface in an audit with interest and penalty attached. It is
the most under-reported line in Indian GST.

Screen the **expense ledger**, not the purchase register. RCM items are usually
booked as expenses, not as purchases, which is exactly why they get missed.

## The two statutory routes

**§9(3) CGST / §5(3) IGST** — notified goods and services where the recipient pays
regardless of the supplier's registration status.

**§9(4) CGST / §5(4) IGST** — notified classes of supply received from an
unregistered person by a specified class of registered person. Narrower than the
original 2017 version, but live for specific categories.

**§9(5)** is different and often confused with RCM: for notified services supplied
through an e-commerce operator, the **operator** is liable as if it were the
supplier. The underlying supplier does not pay. This covers passenger transport by
radio taxi and similar, accommodation below the registration threshold,
housekeeping services, and restaurant services supplied through the platform
(other than from specified premises).

Note also the **TDS-style 2% deduction** on metal scrap supplies between registered
persons introduced at the same time — that is §51 TDS, reported in GSTR-7, not RCM.
Do not conflate the two.

## Compliance mechanics

1. **Self-invoice** under §31(3)(f) — mandatory where the supplier is unregistered.
   Maintain a separate series and report it in GSTR-1 Table 13. Without it the
   credit is exposed even where the tax was paid.
2. **Payment voucher** under §31(3)(g) — issued at the time of payment to the
   supplier.
3. **Time of supply** — earliest of the date of payment, the date of receipt of
   goods (for goods), or 30 days (goods) / 60 days (services) from the supplier's
   invoice date. For associated enterprises abroad, the date of entry in the books
   also counts. Missing this shifts the liability to a later period and attracts
   interest.
4. **Payment in cash.** RCM liability cannot be set off against the credit ledger.
5. **Credit** in the same or a later period, subject to §16 and §17(5), reported in
   Table 4A(3).
6. **Composition taxpayers pay RCM at normal rates**, in cash, with no credit. This
   is a frequent oversight — the composition rate applies to outward supplies only.
7. **RCM applies even to a person registered solely because of §24(iii)** (liability
   to pay under reverse charge), and it applies whether or not the recipient's
   turnover crosses any threshold.

## Screening questions for the expense ledger

Run these against the trial balance every engagement:

- Rent — is any premises leased from an unregistered individual?
- Professional and legal fees — any advocates?
- Freight and cartage — any GTA, and has the GTA opted for forward charge?
- Security and housekeeping charges — is the supplier a body corporate?
- Director remuneration, sitting fees, commission, personal guarantees.
- Software subscriptions, cloud services, advertising platforms, overseas
  consultants, intra-group management charges — all import of services.
- Sponsorship of events.
- Any payment to a government department other than statutory fees.
- Scrap sales and purchases.
- Vehicle hire from a non-corporate supplier.

Record the answer for each, including the negatives. "Checked, none" in the
working paper is evidence; an absent line is not.
