# Fractional CFO playbook

A fractional CFO sells decisions, not reports. The books are the raw material; the product is a founder who knows what to do next month and why. This playbook sequences the work so value shows in 30 days and the engagement does not dissolve into bookkeeping. For deeper frameworks see `cfo-advisor`, `financial-analyst` and `scenario-war-room`.

## Step 0: Scope and fit

1. Confirm the client has a competent bookkeeper or controller; if not, fix the books first (or price that work separately). A CFO on dirty books produces confident wrong numbers.
2. Agree the outcome, not hours: for example "monthly close in 10 days, rolling 13-week cash view, board-ready pack, fundraising readiness in 6 months."
3. Write the boundary: advisory and analysis, not tax filings, audit or legal advice, and not authority to bind the company.
4. Set cadence and price (retainer for the cadence, project fees for transactions); see `accounting-firm-engagement-letters-and-pricing`.

## Days 1 to 30: Diagnose and stabilise

1. Collect: last 24 months of financials, bank and card statements, debt agreements, cap table, major contracts, budget if any, payroll register, tax filings status, KPI definitions, key systems.
2. Run the **finance health check** and score each 0 to 3: close timeliness and accuracy; revenue recognition; cash visibility; AR/AP discipline; payroll and tax compliance; controls and approvals; reporting and KPIs; planning; funding and covenants; systems and data.
3. Rebuild a quick **13-week cash forecast** from bank balance, AR, AP, payroll and known commitments; this is the first deliverable and the proof of value.
4. Compute the baseline: gross margin by product/customer, burn or free cash flow, runway, DSO/DPO/DIO, customer concentration, top cost lines, headcount cost %.
5. Deliver a **one-page diagnostic**: three things that are fine, three that are urgent, three that are strategic, each with an owner and a date.

## Days 31 to 60: Build the operating system

1. **Close process:** calendar, checklist, owners, target close day; use `month-end-close`.
2. **Dashboard:** 8 to 12 KPIs max, each with a definition, source, owner and target; no KPI nobody will act on. Example set: revenue and growth, gross margin, contribution margin by line, cash and runway, DSO, burn multiple (where relevant), headcount cost %, pipeline coverage, churn or repeat rate.
3. **Budget and rolling forecast:** driver-based (volume x price, headcount plan, fixed costs); three cases (base, downside, upside); re-forecast monthly for the next 12 months.
4. **Controls:** approval limits, bank dual authorisation, vendor onboarding, expense policy.
5. **Pricing and unit economics review:** margin by offer, customer, channel; feed to `pricing-and-profitability-analysis`.

## Days 61 to 90: Move to decisions

1. Monthly **CFO meeting** (60 minutes, fixed agenda): last month vs plan, cash and runway, forecast changes, top three decisions needed, risks, next actions. Send the pack 48 hours before; the meeting is for decisions.
2. Quarterly: strategy and capital review (funding, hiring plan, pricing, new markets), scenario work, covenant and investor reporting.
3. Annual: budget, audit or review readiness, tax planning handoff, banking and insurance review.
4. Capital plan: whether to raise, borrow, bootstrap or exit and the timeline; if raising, run `startup-fundraising-readiness`.

## The monthly pack (maximum 8 pages)

1. Executive summary: five lines, what changed and what we need to decide.
2. P&L vs budget and prior year with commentary on variances over threshold (use `flux-variance-analysis`).
3. Cash: actual vs forecast, 13-week outlook, runway.
4. Balance sheet highlights and working capital.
5. KPIs vs targets.
6. Forecast changes and risks.
7. Decision log and action list with owners.

## Working rules

- Numbers first, narrative second; every chart answers "so what do we do".
- Never present numbers you have not tied to the ledger; flag unreconciled areas explicitly.
- Escalate bad news within 24 hours: runway under 6 months, covenant risk, payroll risk, tax arrears, unusual transactions.
- Keep a decision log; reread it at the quarter.
- Share definitions: one KPI definition, one source, forever, or the trend is meaningless.
- Protect confidentiality of cap table, valuation and compensation.

## Failure modes

- Becoming the bookkeeper because nobody else will.
- Dashboard of 40 metrics, none owned.
- Forecast set once and never updated.
- Advice outside competence (legal, tax) with no referral.
- No exit plan: build the client's internal team so a full-time CFO can take over when scale demands.

## Output

Health-check scorecard, one-page diagnostic, 13-week cash forecast, KPI dashboard spec, budget and rolling forecast model, monthly pack template, decision log.
