# Engagement letters and pricing for accounting firms

The engagement letter is the firm's best risk control and its best sales document: it defines what the client buys, what the firm will not do, and what the client must supply. Most disputes, write-offs and malpractice claims trace to an undefined scope.

## Step 1: Choose the service and level of assurance

State exactly one per engagement and do not blur them: bookkeeping and write-up, compilation, review, audit, tax preparation, payroll, CFO advisory, special project. Each carries different standards, independence and documentation requirements (compilation and review standards, audit standards, tax-practitioner rules); name the standards that apply. Independence rules may forbid combining services for the same client; check before offering.

## Step 2: Engagement letter structure

1. **Parties and effective date;** legal names; the entities and periods covered.
2. **Services included:** itemised, with frequency and deliverable (for example "monthly bank reconciliation of up to X accounts and delivery of the reconciled ledger by business day 10").
3. **Services excluded:** state them (audit, legal advice, valuation, investigation of fraud, forensic work, tax planning, representation before tax authorities unless listed, systems selection and implementation, historical clean-up beyond the stated period).
4. **Client responsibilities:** timely, complete and accurate records; responding within set days; management decisions; approving journal entries and reports; maintaining internal controls; notifying the firm of events (new entities, loans, ownership changes).
5. **Records and access:** how documents are shared (secure portal, not email), retention, ownership of client records, return of records.
6. **Fees and billing:** fee model, due dates, late-payment interest, expense pass-through, suspension for non-payment, how out-of-scope work is quoted and approved.
7. **Timeline and delivery:** target dates and the dependence on client timeliness.
8. **Reliance and limitations:** the firm relies on client information and does not audit it; reports are for the client's use; third-party reliance excluded.
9. **Liability limits** where permitted by law and professional rules; confidentiality; data protection and privacy compliance; conflicts; use of technology and AI tools with disclosure of safeguards; subcontractors or offshore teams disclosed.
10. **Term and termination:** renewal, notice period, final billing, transition assistance and file return.
11. **Dispute resolution:** governing law, escalation, mediation or arbitration.
12. **Signatures:** authorised signers for each party, with dated acceptance. No work before signature.
Re-issue the letter when scope, parties or standards change, and renew annually.

## Step 3: Scope control in operation

1. Maintain a scope checklist per client (accounts, entities, volumes, deadlines).
2. Every out-of-scope request produces a short change order: description, hours estimate, fee, approval; work starts after approval. Log requests that were absorbed free; they feed the next price review.
3. Track actual hours against the scope; when overruns exceed a set percentage for two consecutive months, re-scope or reprice.

## Step 4: Build the price

1. Cost-based floor: hours by role x loaded cost rate (salary + burden + overhead share), plus target margin (commonly 25 to 40 percent net of partner time, varying by firm).
2. Estimate hours by driver: transaction volume, number of accounts, entities, payroll frequency and headcount, sales-tax jurisdictions, quality of client records, complexity of the industry (see the industry skills), software.
3. Value check: what the client avoids or gains (cash visibility, tax saved, lender-ready statements, risk reduction); price higher where the value is high and the work is repeatable.
4. Models:
   - **Fixed monthly:** predictable; define volume bands and overage rates.
   - **Tiered packages (for example Essential / Plus / Advisory):** make scope differences visible; reduces custom quotes.
   - **Value-based project:** fixed price against an agreed outcome, with written assumptions; protect with change orders.
   - **Hourly:** for unpredictable or investigative work; show estimates and caps.
5. Quote ranges to the client with what is and is not included; avoid discounting to win unqualified clients.

## Step 5: Annual repricing

Review each client: realised hourly rate, margin, scope drift, record quality, collection history, strategic value. Apply an inflation or cost increase at least annually (give notice per the letter, commonly 30 to 60 days); larger increases for clients below margin target; offer a scope change as an alternative; fire clients who remain unprofitable and non-compliant, with an orderly transition.

## Failure modes

- Starting work on an unsigned letter.
- "Everything accounting" scope.
- Fixed fee with no volume band, so growth erodes margin.
- No client-responsibility clause; delays become the firm's cost.
- Never raising fees, then needing a 30 percent increase.

## Output

Engagement letter template with the clauses above, scope checklist, change-order form, price build-up sheet, client profitability review for repricing.
