# Professional services and agency accounting

The product is people's time, so revenue accuracy depends on time capture and on deciding, contract by contract, whether the firm is principal or agent. Most errors come from one of those two.

## Step 1: Classify each contract

| Contract type | Recognition (ASC 606 / IFRS 15) | Typical balance sheet effect |
|---|---|---|
| Time and materials | As hours are worked at billing rate (right-to-invoice practical expedient) | Unbilled AR (contract asset) until invoiced |
| Fixed fee, project | Over time, by input (hours or cost) or output (milestones) measure | Contract asset / liability from billing vs progress |
| Retainer (monthly, same scope) | Straight-line over the service period | Deferred revenue if billed in advance |
| Retainer with hours bank | Hours used; unused hours expire per contract terms | Breakage recognised only per the contract and policy |
| Success / contingent fee | Only when variable consideration is no longer constrained | Nothing until the constraint lifts |
| Subscription / managed service | Ratably | Deferred revenue |

Write the classification into a contract register; revenue is posted from the register, not from invoices.

## Step 2: Principal vs agent (agencies especially)

Media buys, freelancers and third-party software passed to clients: are you in control of the service before it transfers? Indicators of principal: primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory or pricing discretion, you bear credit risk. If you merely arrange it, report the *net* fee. Gross reporting inflates revenue and shrinks margin percentage; auditors test this. Reimbursable expenses billed at cost with no markup are normally an agent-type pass-through only where the criteria are met; document the judgement.

## Step 3: Time and WIP

1. Daily or weekly timesheets by project and task; lock the period after approval. Missing time is the first leak.
2. Cost rates: loaded cost per hour = (salary + burden) / productive hours. Bill rates from the rate card.
3. Unbilled WIP = approved time and expenses not yet invoiced, valued at bill rate (T&M) or at percent-complete (fixed fee). Age it: over 60 days unbilled is a collection risk and a recognition question.
4. Write-downs and write-offs are separate: pre-bill write-down reduces billed amount (realisation), post-bill write-off is bad debt.
5. Reconcile: timesheet hours x rate = WIP movement; billed = AR movement; project revenue = GL revenue.

## Step 4: Fixed-fee progress

Percent complete by hours: hours incurred / (hours incurred + hours to complete). The hours-to-complete estimate must be refreshed monthly by the project lead. If hours-to-complete exceeds remaining budget, recognise the loss on the contract immediately if the contract becomes onerous. Milestone billing that front-loads cash creates deferred revenue; billing behind progress creates a contract asset.

## Step 5: KPIs

| KPI | Formula |
|---|---|
| Utilisation | Billable hours / available hours (target by role) |
| Realisation | Billed amount / standard value of hours worked |
| Collection | Cash received / billed in period |
| Effective rate | Revenue / hours worked |
| Project margin | (Revenue - direct labour cost - direct expenses) / revenue |
| Leverage | Staff FTE / partner FTE |
| Backlog | Signed, unrecognised contract value |
| WIP days / debtor days | Unbilled / (revenue per day); AR / (revenue per day) |

Review any project below target margin or above 110 percent of budgeted hours at the monthly project review; the question is cause (scope creep, underestimate, rework), not blame.

## Failure modes

- Invoicing as revenue: retainer billed in advance booked all at once.
- Fixed-fee overrun not provisioned until delivery.
- Gross-reporting pass-through media or contractor spend with no control analysis.
- Time entered at month end from memory.
- Expenses not recharged because nobody matches them to a project code.

## Output

Contract register with recognition method, monthly WIP and deferred-revenue roll-forward, project profitability report, utilisation and realisation dashboard, and a principal-vs-agent memo for each significant pass-through.
