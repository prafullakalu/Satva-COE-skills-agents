# Banking relationships and facilities

Banks are suppliers of payments, custody and credit. Manage them like key suppliers: know what you buy, what it costs, what the alternative is, and what the lender needs from you before it needs to ask.

## Step 1: Needs map

List the services required and their volumes: operating accounts per currency, payments (domestic, international, batch, real-time), collections (direct debit, card, lockbox, virtual accounts), cash concentration, FX, credit (revolver, term, overdraft, guarantee, trade finance), cards, merchant acquiring, and digital access (API, host-to-host, ERP integration). Mark each as core or optional and note the current provider.

## Step 2: Bank selection or RFP

1. Define criteria with weights: credit appetite and relationship fit, geographic and currency coverage, payments capability and integration, pricing, service model, balance-sheet strength, covenant and security demands, exit terms.
2. Send the same needs map and volume data to two to four banks; request pricing in a standard grid (account fees, per-transaction, FX spread in bps over a published reference rate, interest on balances, credit margin, commitment fee, arrangement fee).
3. Score on the weighted criteria; do not decide on headline price alone; FX spreads and "free" accounts with high payment fees hide cost.
4. Plan the transition: parallel run, signer and mandate changes, direct-debit and payroll migration, customer notification of new bank details with verification.

## Step 3: Fee analysis (annual)

Take 12 months of bank analysis statements. Group by service; compute unit cost; compare to the RFP grid; challenge any charge not tied to a contracted tariff; reconcile earnings credit or interest offset against fees. Escalate FX spread monitoring: compare executed rates to the mid-market rate at the time of deal.

## Step 4: Credit facilities

| Facility | Use | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Revolver / line of credit | Working-capital swings, seasonal | Commitment fee on undrawn, borrowing base, clean-down requirements |
| Term loan | Capex, acquisitions | Amortisation schedule, prepayment penalty, security |
| Overdraft | Short-term | Repayable on demand, no covenant protection |
| Guarantees / LCs | Trade, leases, tenders | Fees, cash collateral, expiry |
| Asset-based lending | Receivables or inventory backed | Eligibility rules, reporting frequency, dilution |
| Credit cards / cash back | Spend control | Statement-cycle float, limit, interest if not paid in full |

Review each agreement for: interest basis (benchmark plus margin, floors), fees, security and guarantees, financial and non-financial covenants, events of default and cross-default, information undertakings and deadlines, change-of-control and assignment, mandatory prepayments, permitted debt and liens, and dividend restrictions. Hand covenant mechanics to `debt-and-covenant-management`.

## Step 5: KYC and lender package

Keep a ready pack: certified formation documents, ownership chart and beneficial owners, IDs for signers, board resolution for banking mandates, latest audited or reviewed financials, tax IDs, licences, nature of business and expected flows, sanctions and AML policy. Refresh on schedule and on any ownership change. Lender package (monthly or quarterly): management accounts, covenant compliance certificate, aged receivables and payables, cash and 13-week forecast, budget variance, material events, insurance evidence.

## Step 6: Annual relationship review

Per bank: wallet share vs credit commitment, fees paid, service incidents, pricing vs RFP, covenant headroom, key contacts, next maturity. Avoid single-bank dependence for payments or liquidity; keep at least one fallback payment channel and a tested contingency path.

## Failure modes

- Choosing the cheapest account fee and paying double in FX spread.
- Signing a facility without a covenant calendar owner.
- Late lender reporting that triggers technical default.
- Customers paying fraudulent "new bank details".
- KYC lapses freezing accounts.

## Output

Needs map, RFP scorecard, annual fee analysis, facility summary sheet (limit, drawn, margin, maturity, covenants, security), lender reporting calendar, and KYC pack checklist.
