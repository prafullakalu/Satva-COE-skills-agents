# E-E-A-T signals

E-E-A-T comes from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines. It is not a ranking factor or a score; it is a framework describing what Google's systems try to reward. Trust is the most important element; the others support it. You cannot add it with markup. You show it with evidence a reader and a rater can verify.

## The four elements and what evidence looks like
| Element | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | Has the author actually done this? | First-hand examples, original photos or screenshots, real data, specific process detail, "what went wrong" |
| Expertise | Do they have the knowledge? | Credentials, track record, depth beyond the obvious, correct detail |
| Authoritativeness | Are they a go-to source? | Citations and links from respected sources, press, speaking, industry recognition, mentions |
| Trust | Is the page honest, accurate and safe? | Transparent ownership, contact details, sources, corrections, secure site, clear policies, accurate claims |

## Audit workflow
1. **Classify the topic.** YMYL (health, finance, legal, safety, civic) raises the bar; accounting, tax and investing advice is YMYL. Everyday topics need less formal credentials.
2. **Site-level checks.** About page (who, what, where, since when, why trustworthy), named team with bios, visible contact and address, privacy, terms, refund or complaints policy, editorial and fact-checking policy, ownership and funding disclosure, reviews and case studies on independent platforms, HTTPS.
3. **Page-level checks.** Named author with a bio page that shows relevant experience and links to profiles; a reviewer for sensitive content (a qualified accountant for tax articles) with credential and review date; sources linked to primary materials; a visible updated date that reflects real edits; first-hand evidence; accurate, non-exaggerated claims; ads and affiliate relationships disclosed; no misleading "best" claims without method.
4. **Off-site checks.** Google the brand and authors: Business Profile, review sites, news mentions, professional registries, LinkedIn, speaker pages, Wikipedia or Wikidata if notable. Reconcile name, address and description across them. Note negative reputation signals and how they are handled.
5. **Gap list.** For each missing signal, state the fix, owner and effort.
6. **Implement.** Add author and reviewer boxes; build author pages; add Organization and Person schema consistent with visible content (sameAs to real profiles); publish methodology and editorial policy; add case studies with real results (client-approved); retire or fix inaccurate older content.

## Rules
- Never invent credentials, reviews, awards or client logos. Fabricated E-E-A-T is a trust violation and a legal risk.
- Do not use an AI-written page under an invented expert persona. Using AI as a drafting aid is acceptable only with real expert review and accountability.
- Regulated claims (tax, investment, legal) need a qualified reviewer and a jurisdiction note.

## Output
Scorecard by element (strong / adequate / weak with evidence), prioritised fix list, templates for an author bio, reviewer note and editorial policy outline, and a re-check plan (quarterly).

## Do not
Claim "E-E-A-T score". Do not treat schema as a substitute for real credentials.
