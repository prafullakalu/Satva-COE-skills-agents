# LinkedIn-first B2B social content

In B2B, people outperform pages. Aim for a small number of credible voices posting useful, specific things on a steady rhythm, with the company page amplifying.

## 1. Set the frame
- Goal (reach in ICP, inbound conversations, hiring, event signups) and one measure.
- 2-4 voices: founders/leaders, subject-matter experts, client-facing leads. The company page reposts and adds proof.
- 3 content themes mapped to the pillars (see `marketing-content-strategy`), plus one human theme (team, process, behind the scenes).
- Voice per `marketing-brand-voice-guide`; individuals keep their own voice within it.

## 2. Post types that work in B2B
| Type | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Insight/lesson post | A specific thing learned from client work | Anonymise; lead with the lesson |
| Contrarian take | Short POV (see `marketing-thought-leadership`) | Back with a reason |
| How-to / checklist | Steps the reader can apply | Best for saves and shares |
| Carousel / PDF | Frameworks, before/after, process | 6-10 slides, one idea per slide |
| Case snippet | Result story (permissioned) | Numbers with baseline |
| Data post | One chart and its meaning | Source it |
| Event/webinar | Promotion, live notes, recap | Clip or quote graphic |
| Poll | Market research, with follow-up post | Max 1 a month |
| Short video | Explaining a screen or concept in 60-90 sec | Captions always |

## 3. Anatomy of a post
- **Hook (line 1-2, <= ~140 chars before "see more"):** a concrete claim, number, mistake or question the ICP recognises. No "I'm excited to announce".
- **Body:** short paragraphs, one idea each, plain words, specific details. White space; no walls of text. 
- **Payoff:** the lesson or step the reader can use.
- **Close:** a genuine question, or a soft CTA. One link maximum, and consider placing it in the first comment or a document post, as outbound links can reduce reach.
- Length: 150-300 words for text posts is a good default; test.
- Hashtags: 0-3 relevant ones; no tag pile. Tag people only when they have agreed or are genuinely referenced.

Hook formulas: "Most [role] do X. Here is why it breaks at [scale]." / "We reviewed [N] [things]. [Surprising finding]." / "A client asked [question]. The honest answer:" / "[Number] mistakes in [process], ranked by cost."

## 4. Cadence and calendar
- Personal profiles: 2-4 posts a week each; company page 3-5 a week.
- Mix per week: 2 insight/how-to, 1 proof (case, data), 1 human or event, 1 reactive slot.
- Batch: record one SME session, produce 5-8 posts. Schedule, but be present live for the first 60 minutes to reply.
- Repurpose: blog -> 3 posts + carousel; webinar -> clips + quotes; case study -> snippet + quote graphic; customer question -> insight post.

## 5. Engagement
- Reply to every comment in the first day with substance; ask follow-up questions.
- Comment daily on 5-10 posts from ICP accounts and peers (add something, not "great post").
- Connection requests with a one-line reason; do not pitch in the first message.
- DMs: respond helpfully; no copy-paste sales sequences. Respect LinkedIn terms: no scraped automation, no pods or fake engagement.

## 6. Compliance and safety
No client names, data, screenshots with identifiers or confidential numbers without written approval. Check employer social policy and regulated-claims rules (financial/tax advice disclaimers where relevant). Correct errors publicly and quickly.

## 7. Measure (monthly)
Per post: impressions, engagement rate, saves/shares, profile visits, followers in ICP, inbound DMs, meetings booked and pipeline touched (ask "how did you hear about us?"). Rank formats and themes; double down on the top third; retire the bottom third. Impressions alone are vanity.

## Output
A weekly plan table (date, voice, theme, format, hook, CTA, asset), 3-5 ready-to-post drafts with two hook options each, a carousel slide-by-slide outline when asked, and the measurement sheet. Draft first-person posts in the author's voice and flag anything needing approval.

## Other B2B channels (brief)
X for real-time industry conversation; YouTube for searchable how-tos; communities and Slack groups for peer help; Reddit only with transparent participation. Pick one extra channel at most until LinkedIn is steady.

## Failure modes
- Corporate page announcements nobody reads.
- Engagement bait ("Agree?") without substance.
- Posting in bursts then vanishing.
- Writing for LinkedIn peers rather than buyers.
- Unapproved client details.
