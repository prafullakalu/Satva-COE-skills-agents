# Competitive analysis

Goal: understand how buyers choose and where you can credibly win. Not a feature checklist.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the set (3 tiers)
- **Direct:** same solution, same buyer.
- **Indirect:** different solution to the same problem (in-house team, spreadsheets, an ERP module, a freelancer).
- **Status quo:** doing nothing. Often the biggest competitor in B2B; always include it.
Cap at 5-7 profiled competitors. Pick by how often they appear in your deals, not by brand size.

### 2. Collect evidence, with citations
Sources, in order of reliability: your own won/lost notes and call recordings; customer interviews; competitor pricing and docs pages; job postings (reveal strategy and stack); review sites (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot); changelogs, release notes; case studies and partner directories; public filings or funding news; web archive snapshots for messaging changes.
Rules: record the URL and the date retrieved for every fact. Distinguish **[Observed]** (public, quotable), **[Reported]** (from a customer or rep), **[Inferred]** (your reading). Never present inferred as observed. Use only public or legitimately obtained information; no impersonating prospects, no scraping behind logins, no confidential leaks.

### 3. Profile each competitor (one page)
Positioning claim and target buyer; offer and packaging; pricing model (published or range, with source); strengths buyers actually cite; weaknesses buyers actually cite; proof they lean on; channels and content themes; recent moves (90 days); where they win against you and where you win against them.

### 4. Build the comparison matrix
Rows = buying criteria **taken from how buyers decide** (from call notes), not from your feature list. Columns = you + competitors. Cells: Strong / Adequate / Weak / Unknown with a source footnote. Weight the criteria by importance to the ICP. Show the matrix honestly; include rows where you lose.

### 5. Find the gaps
- **Claims everyone makes** (table stakes: do not lead with them).
- **Claims nobody makes** that buyers care about (white space).
- **Claims you can prove and they cannot** (differentiators).
- **Where you are exposed** and the honest response.

### 6. Turn it into assets (only what is asked)
- **Battlecard:** one page per competitor: quick summary, when we win, when we lose, landmine questions to ask, objection handling, proof points, "do not say" list.
- **Alternatives/vs page brief:** fair tone, factual table, clear who each option suits. Pages that trash competitors lose trust and invite legal letters; do not make unprovable claims or use competitor trademarks beyond nominative reference.
- **Messaging inputs:** hand gaps to `marketing-positioning-messaging`.

### 7. Set a refresh cadence
Light monitoring monthly (pricing page diffs, release notes, new case studies, hiring); full refresh twice a year or after a major competitor event.

## Output format
1. Executive summary (5 bullets: where we win, where we lose, the biggest threat, the biggest opening, recommended moves).
2. Competitor set table with tier.
3. Profiles.
4. Weighted matrix with sources.
5. Gaps and recommendations ranked by impact and effort.
6. Evidence log (claim, source, date, label).

## Failure modes
- Feature-count comparisons that favour whoever lists the most checkboxes.
- Stale pricing quoted as current; always date it.
- Ignoring the status quo and in-house options.
- Hiding weaknesses in a document the sales team will rely on.
- Assuming a competitor's marketing claim is true; verify against reviews and docs.
