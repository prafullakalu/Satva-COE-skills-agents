# ICP and buyer personas

An ICP is a filter on companies. A persona is a model of people inside them. Build the ICP first, from outcomes, then personas for the roles that decide.

## Workflow

### 1. Gather evidence
Ask for: closed-won and closed-lost lists, retention/expansion by account, deal size and cycle length, sales call recordings or notes, support themes, 5-10 customer interviews. No data? Run a minimal interview plan (step 7) and label results `[Hypothesis]`.

### 2. Score historic customers
For each won account record: industry, size (revenue/headcount/order volume), tech stack, geography, trigger event, sales cycle, ACV, time to value, retained at 12 months, referrals given. Rank by **LTV / cost-to-serve**, not by revenue alone. The top quartile is your ICP seed; the bottom quartile (churned, discounted, high support) is your anti-ICP.

### 3. Find the patterns
Look for what the top quartile shares and the bottom lacks. Separate:
- **Firmographics:** industry, size band, region, ownership model.
- **Technographics:** systems they run (ERP, e-commerce platform, accounting package, marketplace channels). For integration services this is often the sharpest filter.
- **Situational triggers:** new funding, new sales channel, new finance lead, audit, system migration, month-end pain, compliance deadline.
- **Disqualifiers:** budget floor, regulated sectors you cannot serve, custom-everything buyers.

### 4. Write the ICP (one page)
```
ICP: [segment name]
Firmographics: ...
Technographics: ...
Trigger events (ranked): ...
Disqualifiers: ...
Tiering: Tier 1 = all firmographics + a trigger; Tier 2 = fits, no trigger; Tier 3 = adjacent, nurture only
Evidence: n accounts, source, date
```
Keep to one primary ICP and at most one secondary. State the size of the addressable list (count of accounts you can name) so the filter is checkable.

### 5. Map the buying committee
Typically: **economic buyer** (owns budget), **champion** (feels the pain daily), **technical evaluator**, **blocker/risk owner** (security, finance, legal), **end user**. For each: what they fear, what proof they need, which channel reaches them.

### 6. Write personas (only for roles that influence the deal)
Per persona, 1 page max:
- Role, reports to, what they are measured on.
- **Job to be done** in the form "When [situation], I want to [action], so I can [outcome]".
- Top 3 pains in their own words (quote real ones).
- Buying criteria and top objections.
- Information diet: where they read, who they trust, what content format they accept.
- Message that lands / message that backfires.
Never include invented demographics (age, hobbies, stock photo name) unless data supports them; they steer copy toward stereotypes.

### 7. If you must interview (minimum viable)
8 interviews, 30 minutes: recent buyers (4), lost deals (2), churned (2). Questions: what happened just before you started looking, what else did you try, who was in the room, what almost stopped you, what would you tell a peer. Ask for past behaviour, never hypotheticals.

## Output
ICP one-pager, tiering rule, anti-ICP list, committee map table, 2-4 personas, and a "data gaps" list. Add how each artifact is used: list-building filters for sales, targeting for ads, topic selection for content.

## Failure modes
- ICP defined by who you wish bought, not who stays.
- Ten personas nobody uses; cap at the roles that change the message.
- Demographic personas instead of situation-and-job personas.
- Treating the user as the buyer in committee sales.
- No review date; re-run the analysis at least every two quarters or after a big segment shift.
- Pasting customer names or confidential figures into shared docs; anonymise.
