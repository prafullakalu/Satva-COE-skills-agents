# Content strategy and editorial calendar

Content is an asset portfolio with a job. Start from the business goal and the buyer's questions, then size the plan to the team that will actually ship it.

## Workflow

### 1. Fix the goal and capacity
Ask: the one business outcome (qualified pipeline, sales-cycle shortening, retention, hiring, authority in a niche), current baseline, available writers/SMEs/designers, weekly hours, budget. Plan to 70% of stated capacity; calendars fail from over-commitment.

### 2. Audit what exists (if anything)
Inventory URL, topic, format, date, traffic, conversions, links, last update. Mark each: **Keep / Update / Merge / Retire**. Content that is old, thin and unvisited is cheaper to merge or redirect than to rewrite. Gaps = buyer questions with no strong asset.

### 3. Choose 3-4 pillars
Each pillar = a topic where you have real expertise, buyers have a problem, and you can credibly sell. Example for an accounting-integration firm: multichannel accounting automation; e-commerce-to-ledger reconciliation; month-end close for retailers; AI agents in finance operations. Each pillar gets a hub page plus clusters.

### 4. Build the topic list from demand, not brainstorm
Sources: sales questions and objections, support tickets, call transcripts, "People also ask", forum threads, competitor top pages, search data if available (hand keyword depth to the SEO skills), customer interview language. For each topic note: stage, intent, primary persona, proof/SME available, conversion path.

### 5. Match format to funnel stage
| Stage | Buyer question | Formats | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem aware | "Why is this happening?" | Guides, data posts, POV articles, short social | Subscribe, related guide |
| Solution aware | "What are my options?" | Comparisons, checklists, webinars, templates | Download, register |
| Vendor aware | "Why you?" | Case studies, how-it-works, ROI notes, demos | Talk to us |
| Customer | "How do I get more?" | Playbooks, release notes, community | Expand, refer |
Ship a balance: roughly 50% top and middle, 30% bottom, 20% customer for a services firm; adjust to the funnel leak you measured.

### 6. Build the calendar
Columns: publish date, working title, pillar, stage, persona, format, primary keyword or hook, owner, SME, status (idea / briefed / drafting / review / scheduled / live), CTA, distribution plan, update-by date.
Rules: one flagship asset per month and derive 6-10 pieces from it (slices, quotes, charts, email, talk track); batch interviews with SMEs; leave 15% of slots open for reactive pieces; plan refreshes as first-class items.

### 7. Define distribution before drafting
For each piece: owned (site, newsletter), social (see `marketing-linkedin-b2b-content`), sales enablement, partner channels, paid boost if justified. A piece with no distribution plan is not ready.

### 8. Measure
Leading: publish rate vs plan, time from brief to live. Engagement: qualified visits, newsletter signups, saves. Outcome: influenced pipeline, assisted conversions, sales-reported usage. Review monthly (what to double down on / cut) and rewrite the plan quarterly.

## Output
Strategy one-pager (goal, audience, pillars, formats, cadence, KPIs), audit table, topic backlog scored by impact x effort, a 90-day calendar table, and a brief template link to `marketing-blog-production`.

## Failure modes
- Publishing to a calendar for its own sake; cut weak slots.
- Pillars matching what you can write, not what buyers need.
- No SME time reserved: content turns generic.
- No update cycle: old pages decay and mislead.
- Counting pageviews as success for a pipeline goal.
