# Webinar and event content

A webinar is a content asset with a registration funnel. Plan backwards from the follow-up and the pipeline action you want.

## 1. Define the goal and the audience
One goal: pipeline conversations, customer education, partner co-marketing, or authority. Set targets: registrations, attendance rate (typical on-demand-inclusive 35-45% live), engaged minutes, meetings booked. One persona, one problem. If two audiences need two different talks, run two sessions.

## 2. Topic and title
Choose a problem the audience is actively trying to solve, with a result they can apply the same day. Title formula: **[Outcome] for [who]: [method or proof]**. E.g. "Cut reconciliation time for multichannel sellers: a live walkthrough of payout matching". Avoid "Introduction to" and "The future of". Test the title with three people in the ICP or against sales conversations.

## 3. Speakers and format
- 1 expert plus 1 host/moderator; add a customer for credibility (permissioned, prepared).
- Formats: demo-led walkthrough, expert panel, fireside chat, workshop, customer story, Q&A clinic.
- Length: 30-45 min content plus 10-15 min Q&A. Weekday mid-morning in the audience's time zone; offer on-demand.
- Speaker brief: goal, audience, 3 takeaways, outline with timings, slide template, tech check slot, no-go topics (confidential client data, roadmap promises, pricing commitments).

## 4. Abstract and landing page copy
- 2-3 sentence promise stating the problem and the outcome.
- 3 "you will learn" bullets, each specific and verifiable in the session.
- Speaker bios with one line of relevance each.
- Who it is for / not for. Date, time zone, duration, replay policy.
- Registration form: minimal fields (name, work email, company); add one qualifying question only if sales will use it. State how data will be used and honour consent rules (GDPR, CAN-SPAM, local law).

## 5. Run-of-show (45 min example)
| Min | Segment | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Welcome, housekeeping, agenda, poll | Host |
| 3-8 | The problem, with real data or a story | Expert |
| 8-30 | Method or live demo, 3 key steps | Expert |
| 30-35 | Customer evidence or example | Customer/expert |
| 35-43 | Q&A (seed 2 questions in case the room is quiet) | Host |
| 43-45 | Recap, single CTA, thanks | Host |
Rehearse once with timing and tech; record a backup; confirm screen-share, audio and fallback.

## 6. Slide principles
One idea per slide; headline states the takeaway; no more than ~25 words; show real screens or numbers; one slide with the CTA and a clean URL; leave sensitive data out of screenshots (mask client names, IDs, amounts if real).

## 7. Promotion plan (4 weeks)
| When | Asset |
|---|---|
| T-4 weeks | Landing page live; LinkedIn announcement; sales and partner kit |
| T-3 | Invite email to segment; speaker posts |
| T-2 | Reminder with a teaser insight; customer-list invite |
| T-1 week | Last-chance post; calendar reminder |
| T-1 day / T-1 hour | Reminder emails with join link |
| T+1 day | Replay to attendees and no-shows (different subject lines) |
Write subject lines around the outcome, not the event. Track registrations by source.

## 8. Follow-up and repurposing
- Within 24 hours: replay, slides, resource, one CTA. Segment attendees by engagement (watched >50%, asked a question, clicked CTA) and hand hot ones to sales with context.
- Repurpose: blog recap, 5-8 clips or quotes, FAQ from Q&A, checklist from the method, newsletter, a short on-demand version.
- Debrief within a week: metrics vs target, what the audience asked, what to change.

## Output
Event brief, title options, abstract and landing copy, run-of-show table, speaker brief, promotion schedule with drafted copy, follow-up sequence, repurposing list and a KPI sheet.

## Failure modes
- Product pitch disguised as education; keep 80% teaching.
- No rehearsal; dead air in Q&A.
- Collecting registrants and never following up.
- Too many CTAs.
- Promising roadmap items or prices on the record.
