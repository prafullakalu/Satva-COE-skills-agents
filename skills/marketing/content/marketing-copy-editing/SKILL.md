# Copy editing

Edit in descending order of importance. Fixing commas in a page with the wrong message wastes the day.

## Method: six passes
Run the passes in order; list issues per pass, then apply.

### Pass 1: Message
Is there one clear point for one reader? State the point in a sentence. If you cannot, say so and stop; send it back to positioning. Check the promise in the headline is delivered in the body, and the CTA matches the reader's stage.

### Pass 2: Structure
Does the order follow the reader's questions (what is this, is it for me, does it work, what do I do)? Move the strongest material up, cut repetition, split sections that hold two ideas, merge ones that hold half an idea. Check headings tell the story alone.

### Pass 3: Clarity
- Replace abstract nouns with actions ("enable reconciliation automation" -> "match payouts to orders automatically").
- One idea per sentence; sentence length mostly under 25 words; vary rhythm.
- Define or remove jargon; expand acronyms once.
- Cut hedges ("very", "really", "quite", "somewhat") and filler openings ("It is important to note that").
- Active voice unless the actor is irrelevant.
- Fix ambiguous pronouns ("this", "it") by naming the thing.

### Pass 4: Voice
Check against the brand voice guide if provided (`marketing-brand-voice-guide`); otherwise infer from the strongest existing pages and mark as assumed. Ensure consistency of person (we/you), tense, terminology (one name per concept) and tone for the audience.

### Pass 5: Proof and claims
List every claim and check it has: a number with baseline and period, a named source, a quote, or a verifiable fact. Flag unverified superlatives and absolute words ("always", "never", "guarantee", "#1"). Regulated or comparative claims go to the owner for sign-off. Do not add facts yourself.

### Pass 6: Line edit and proof
Spelling (pick one variant, US or UK, and stick to it), grammar, punctuation, number style, date/currency format, capitalisation of product names, consistent hyphenation, link text that says where it goes, alt text, title and meta length. Read the final text aloud.

## Output format
1. **Verdict** (2 lines): what works, the biggest problem.
2. **Priority changes** table: Pass | Location | Issue | Fix | Impact (H/M/L).
3. **Revised copy** (clean).
4. **Tracked changes** only if asked; otherwise a short "what changed and why" list.
5. **Open questions** for the author (claims to verify, missing proof).

## Rules
- Preserve meaning, facts and deliberate style; ask before changing a claim.
- Do not flatten a distinctive voice into corporate neutral.
- Do not shorten by deleting substance; shorten by tightening.
- Keep edits explainable; if you changed something, you must be able to say why.
- Mark things you cannot verify; never "fix" a number by guessing.

## Failure modes
- Rewriting the author's argument instead of editing it.
- Polishing a draft that needs a new thesis.
- Introducing new claims or adjectives while "improving" energy.
