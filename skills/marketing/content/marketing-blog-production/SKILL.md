# Blog and article production workflow

Quality comes from the brief and the edit, not the draft. Work in stages and stop at each gate if inputs are missing.

## Stage 1: Brief (always produce this first)
```
Working title / angle:
Reader (persona, awareness level):
Reader's question in their words:
Search intent + primary keyword (+ 3-5 secondary):  [if SEO-led]
Thesis (one sentence the piece must prove):
Why us / unique input (data, experience, SME view):
Outline (H2s):
Sources to use / must-cite:
Must include (examples, screenshots, numbers):
Must avoid (claims, competitors, topics):
CTA (one) and next-step asset:
Length range, format, owner, SME, due dates:
```
Check the SERP or existing coverage: what do the top results already say, and what will this piece add that they do not (original data, a worked example, a stronger opinion, a template)? If the honest answer is "nothing", change the angle or drop the piece.

## Stage 2: SME input
Collect 20-30 minutes of expert time: 5 targeted questions, record with consent, keep their phrasing, ask for a real story with numbers and a mistake. Without SME input or first-hand data, expect generic output; flag it.

## Stage 3: Outline
- Title: promise + specificity; a number or "how/why" only if it is true to the content.
- Intro in 3 moves: the reader's problem, the stake, what they will get. No throat-clearing, no history of the universe.
- Each H2 answers one sub-question; order by reader need, not by your product tour.
- Plan one concrete asset per 400-600 words: example, table, checklist, screenshot, formula, quote.
- Place the CTA where the reader's problem is solved, not at the opening.

## Stage 4: Draft
- Lead sections with the answer, then explain.
- Define terms on first use; write for a smart outsider.
- Show the work: worked examples with numbers (e.g. how a mismatched payout creates a reconciliation difference and how to trace it).
- Every factual claim gets a source or is first-hand and labelled as such. Link to primary sources, not summaries.
- AI-assisted drafts: treat as untrusted. Verify every number, date, quote and product claim; remove generic filler; inject first-hand detail; keep a disclosure note if your policy requires it.

## Stage 5: Edit (three passes)
1. **Structure:** thesis proven? order right? anything to cut? (Cut the weakest 15%.)
2. **Line:** active voice, shorter sentences, kill hedges and clichés, one idea per paragraph.
3. **Proof and risk:** fact-check against sources; confirm legal, tax or compliance statements with an owner; confirm customer mentions are permissioned; remove confidential detail.

## Stage 6: SEO and publish checklist
- [ ] Title tag <= 60 chars, meta description <= 155, one H1, descriptive H2s.
- [ ] Primary keyword in H1, first 100 words, one H2, URL slug; no stuffing.
- [ ] Internal links: 3-5 to relevant pillar and product pages; external links to authoritative sources.
- [ ] Images with descriptive alt text and compressed; charts have text equivalents.
- [ ] Schema (Article/FAQ) if the CMS supports it.
- [ ] CTA, tracking parameters, author bio with credentials, publish and "last updated" dates.
- [ ] Distribution: newsletter, LinkedIn post and sales enablement note drafted.

## Stage 7: After publish
Review at 30 and 90 days: impressions, clicks, time on page, conversions, assisted pipeline. Update, merge or retire; set a next review date.

## Output
Deliver in order: brief (for approval), outline (for approval), full draft with source list and `[CHECK]` flags, then the publish checklist filled in. Do not skip approval gates when the user asked for staged work.

## Failure modes
- Starting with a keyword and no point of view.
- Padding to a word count.
- Unsourced statistics.
- A product pitch in every paragraph.
- Publishing without a distribution plan or an update date.
