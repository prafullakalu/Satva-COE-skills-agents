# Customer story and testimonial gathering

Proof is the scarcest input in B2B copy. Build a repeatable process rather than begging once.

## 1. Pick the right moments to ask
Best triggers: go-live completed; a metric moved (hours saved, error rate down); positive unsolicited feedback; a renewal or expansion; a successful support escalation; the end of a project phase; an NPS 9-10 response. Never ask during an open incident, an overdue invoice or a dispute.

## 2. Identify candidates
Query the CRM/project records for: delivered projects in the last 6-12 months, health score green, a named champion, a measurable result, and a segment you want more of. Rank by ICP fit and result strength. Ask the account owner to warm the introduction; a request from a stranger gets ignored.

## 3. Make the ask easy
Offer three tiers, lowest friction first:
1. **One-click review** on a review site (G2, Capterra, Google), with the direct link.
2. **Three-question quote** by email or a short call.
3. **Full case study interview** (see `marketing-case-study`).
Send a 4-sentence request: why them, what you need, how long it takes, what they get (a draft to approve, a backlink, an introduction, a donation to a cause they choose; never pay for a review or condition service on one).

Template (adapt):
```
Hi [Name], [Project] has been live for [period] and [specific result they mentioned]. 
Would you be open to sharing two or three sentences about the experience we could quote? 
I can draft it from your feedback so you only need to edit and approve; it takes five minutes.
```

## 4. The short question set
1. What problem were you trying to solve?
2. What changed after we worked together? (number or before/after)
3. What would you say to someone considering us?
Optional: what almost stopped you from choosing us? (reveals objections to answer in copy).

## 5. Edit with integrity
- Keep their words; trim and fix grammar only; show the final text and get written approval.
- Tag each quote with: name, role, company (or anonymised descriptor), date, approved channels, expiry.
- Prefer quotes that state a result or an objection overcome over "great team".
- Do not fabricate, merge or embellish quotes. Do not incentivise ratings in ways the platform or law forbids, and disclose any material connection where required (FTC and local rules).

## 6. Build the proof library
A shared sheet with columns: ID, customer descriptor, segment, service, quote, metric (baseline, result, period), approved channels, approval date, expiry/review date, asset links (case study, video, review URL), owner. Tag by objection answered (price, risk, speed, integration, trust) so copywriters and sales can pull the right proof fast.

## 7. Keep it flowing
Add a standing step to the delivery checklist: at project close, the account manager triggers the ask; marketing reviews the library monthly; target a number per quarter (e.g. 5 quotes, 2 reviews, 1 case study). Thank every contributor and show them where their words appeared.

## 8. Handle refusals and risk
Decline gracefully, offer an anonymised option, record the answer. Check that quotes and logos are not used past their approved scope. Remove on request promptly.

## Output
Candidate list with ranking logic, outreach messages, question set, approval form text, filled library entries and a quarterly proof target.

## Failure modes
- Asking only when it is convenient for you, not at a high point.
- Vague quotes with no outcome.
- Missing written approval.
- Letting the library go stale and quoting expired claims.
