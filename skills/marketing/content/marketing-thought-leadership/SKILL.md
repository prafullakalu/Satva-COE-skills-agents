# Thought leadership and POV pieces

Thought leadership is a claim someone could disagree with, backed by something only you have seen. A summary of trends is not thought leadership.

## 1. Find the stance
Ask the author (or SME) the questions that surface a view:
- What does the industry believe that you think is wrong or incomplete?
- What do you see in client work that is not in the analyst reports?
- What would you do differently if you were a buyer?
- What prediction would you bet on, and what would prove you wrong?
Turn the answer into a one-sentence thesis in the form "Most [group] think X; in practice Y, because Z." If you cannot write it, there is no piece yet.

## 2. Test the thesis (all four)
- **Contestable:** a reasonable peer could disagree.
- **Evidenced:** you hold at least one of: first-hand cases, original data, a reproducible method, a credible primary source.
- **Relevant:** buyers in the ICP feel the consequence.
- **Safe to say:** no confidential client detail, no unfounded attack on a named vendor, no legal or compliance exposure. If unsure, escalate before drafting.

## 3. Choose the form
| Form | Length | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| POV article on own site | 900-1500 | Core argument, SEO and sales reuse |
| LinkedIn essay or post | 150-400 | Test the idea, start conversation |
| Exec byline / guest article | per outlet | Borrowed audience; follow outlet guidelines |
| Data-led report | 1500+ | You have original numbers |
| Prediction piece | 600-1000 | Time-bound, revisit later |

## 4. Structure (argument shape)
1. **Hook:** the contrarian claim or the surprising observation, within two lines.
2. **Common belief:** state the consensus fairly. Do not straw-man.
3. **Why it breaks:** evidence. A specific anonymised story (what happened, what it cost), a number with source, or a mechanism explained step by step.
4. **Your model:** a named framework or checklist the reader can use (3-5 elements).
5. **Counterargument:** the best objection and your honest answer; concede what is true.
6. **Implication:** what a reader should do on Monday.
7. **Close:** a sharp last line and a soft CTA (conversation, resource), not a pitch.

## 5. Interview-to-draft method (ghostwriting)
30-minute recorded interview, 6 questions: the stance, the story that convinced you, the data you have seen, the objection you hear, the advice you give clients, the prediction. Transcribe; keep the author's phrases and cadence; the author reads and edits the draft before anyone else sees it. The byline must reflect views the person actually holds and will defend in public.

## 6. Quality bar
- Original: remove any paragraph that appears in ten other articles.
- Concrete: replace abstractions with cases, numbers, steps.
- Credible: cite primary sources; label opinion as opinion and data as data.
- Consistent with brand voice (`marketing-brand-voice-guide`).
- Written at the level of a smart peer, no hype words, no buzzword stacking ("AI-powered digital transformation").

## 7. Amplify
Slice into 3-5 social posts, a short video script, an email to customers, a talking point for sales, and a conference abstract. Respond to comments within 24 hours; the debate is part of the value.

## Output
Thesis statement, evidence list with gaps, outline, draft with `[CHECK]` flags on every claim, and a derivative-content list.

## Failure modes
- Trend roundups dressed as insight.
- Safe, consensus claims that nobody would dispute.
- Attacking competitors or clients by name.
- Predictions with no timeframe or measure.
- Ghostwritten views the executive would not repeat on a call.
